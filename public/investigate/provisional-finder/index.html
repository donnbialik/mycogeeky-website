1Whose collections, which name

Pulls iNaturalist observations that have both a Provisional Species Name and a DNA Barcode ITS sequence, then sizes up each name across all of iNat: how rare it is, who else has collected it, whether specimens were kept, how well it’s photographed, and when it fruits.

Filters

Data and photos from iNaturalist, through its public API. This tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by iNaturalist. Photos belong to their photographers and are shown under their Creative Commons licenses, credited on each photo; photos marked “all rights reserved” are not shown here and can be viewed on iNaturalist.

Live data from the iNaturalist API. Only observations with both the “Provisional Species Name” and “DNA Barcode ITS” observation fields are counted. Names are matched exactly as typed in the field. “Collections” means observations uploaded by the usernames you entered. Regions come from iNat’s place records (state or province), falling back to the observation’s place text outside North America. Each name’s results are kept in this browser for as long as you choose (1 day by default); use “Save results to a file” to keep or share a search. Sequence checks are simple screens (length and ambiguous bases), not a quality verdict. Requests are spaced about one per second to stay within iNat’s limits, so large searches take a few minutes.

Glossary

Sequenced
How many observations on all of iNat carry this exact provisional name and an ITS sequence. Lower means rarer. The default sort puts the rarest first.
Yours
How many of those came from the usernames you searched.
Collectors
Different iNat accounts that uploaded a sequenced collection of this name. Several collectors usually means separate collection events and more material to study.
Vouchered
Collections with a specimen record: a filled-in voucher, herbarium or fungarium field, or “Voucher Specimen Taken: Yes”. A new species needs a kept specimen for its holotype.
Photos / obs
Average photos per sequenced observation of the name, as a rough measure of how well the macro features are documented.
Regions
US states, Canadian provinces, or countries elsewhere, where it has been collected.
Season
Sequenced observations by month, January to December.
Sequences
Sequences that may need a look: shorter than 350 bases or more than 2% ambiguous bases. Pasted FASTA headers are ignored. Orange when at least a quarter of a name’s sequences are tagged.
Flags
Taxon differs: some observations are filed under a different species-level taxon than the field says. Likely described: at least half are identified as a formally named species, so the code may already be described (fewer than half shows as “N IDed as named sp.”, usually misidentifications). Taxon inactive: iNat’s provisional taxon was retired or merged. ID update: at least 10% of observations have “ID Update Needed: Yes”.
Research candidates
Preset: 2–10 sequenced collections, at least one vouchered, at least two collectors. Use the filters to set your own rules.
