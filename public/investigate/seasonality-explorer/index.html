How to read this, and what it can't tell you
  • These are counts of observations, not of mushrooms. They show when people photograph a species, which follows fruiting but also follows weekends, weather and how many observers are out.
  • Adjusted for effort divides by every observation in a comparison group made in the same place and period, with the same filters. For fungi the default group is all fungi with the main lichen groups left out (Lecanoromycetes, Arthoniomycetes, Lichinomycetes, Verrucariales, Pyrenulales, Trypetheliales), because lichens are photographed all year. You can switch the group to the taxon's own order or class.
  • Two flushes are reported when a second peak is at least 15% as tall as the main one, the dip between them falls below half the smaller peak, the peaks are at least 8 weeks apart, and each side has 20 or more observations and at least a tenth of the total. These cutoffs are rules of thumb.
  • Latitude warnings use the bounding box of the chosen place, so a place with outlying territory can trigger one. The hemisphere check counts the actual observations south of the equator.
  • Confidence is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee. Under 30 observations no season is stated, because the start and end dates would each rest on fewer than three records. Above that, the tool rebuilds the data 400 times from randomly re-picked years (or with random counting noise when there are under 5 years) and reports how far the season edges move. It is also held back when there are fewer than 100 observations, fewer than 5 observers, one observer supplying over half, fewer than 3 years, or one year supplying over 60%.
  • Plausible range lines on the bars are 90% ranges from counting noise alone: a bar of 4 could easily have been 1 or 9. Real data is lumpier than that (one foray can add ten records), so treat the ranges as the minimum uncertainty.
  • DNA-sequenced only checks that the DNA Barcode ITS field is filled in. It does not check that the sequence was reviewed or that the identification matches it.
  • Identifications on iNaturalist can be wrong, especially for species that need microscopy or sequencing. Research grade means two people agreed, not that it was verified.
